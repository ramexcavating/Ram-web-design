## Section — What we do

**Heading:** `Trenchless Scope`

- **Auger boring** — steel casing installed by auger bore with the product pipe carried inside
- **Highway crossings** — under provincial highway and municipal road without lane closure or road cut
- **Rail crossings** — under active rail to railway requirements and inside railway permit conditions
- **Watercourse crossings** — under creeks and rivers with no in-stream work and no bed or bank disturbance

- **Grade-critical bores** — gravity sewer crossings where line and grade have to be held across the bore

## Section — Why trenchless

**Heading:** `When Trenchless Beats Open Cut`

```
Trenchless is not always the cheaper option on the bore itself. It is very
often the cheaper option on the project.

**Highways and busy roads.** An open cut across a provincial highway means
traffic management, permits, lane closures, a pavement cut that becomes a
maintenance liability, and public disruption. A bore means none of it.

**Rail.** Railways rarely permit an open cut across a live line. A bore is
frequently the only option that will be approved at all.

**Watercourses.** An open-cut crossing means in-stream work, a fisheries
timing window, sediment control on a watercourse, isolation and dewatering of
the channel, bed and bank reinstatement, and a much harder permit. Boring
under the creek removes the in-stream work entirely — and with it most of the
environmental risk and most of the permitting.

**Existing surfaces and structures.** Under a paved yard, a parking area, a
runway, mature trees or a structure, boring avoids reinstatement that can cost
more than the pipe.

**Contaminated ground.** Not opening the ground means not handling, testing
and disposing of contaminated material.

If you are weighing a crossing method, call us before the design is fixed.
The comparison is worth doing properly, and it depends on ground conditions,
crossing length and the permitting path — not on a rule of thumb.
```

## Section — What we need to know

**Heading:** `Pricing a Crossing`

```
To price a bore accurately we need a few things. Having them ready saves a
round trip:

- Crossing length and what is being crossed
- Carrier pipe size, material and whether it is gravity or pressure
- Required line and grade tolerance — a gravity sewer is a different job from a water line
- Ground conditions and depth to water table, and any geotechnical or borehole information
- Depth of cover required by the owner of what you're crossing
- Space available for bore and receiving pits on each side
- Any permits already in place, and the timing window if there is one

If you have some of that and not the rest, call anyway. We would rather help
you scope it than wait for a complete package.
```

That section does two useful things: it makes you look like you know the work, and it gets an estimator on the phone with you instead of emailing a tender to five contractors.
