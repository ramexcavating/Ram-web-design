## Section — What we do

**Heading:** `Earthworks Scope`

- **Bulk excavation** — mass excavation and removal for building footprints, basements, ponds and site regrading
- **Mass grading** — cut and fill to design grade across whole sites, balanced on site wherever the material allows
- **Structural fill placement** — engineered fill placed in specified lifts, compacted and tested to the geotechnical specification
- **Building pads and equipment pads** — prepared, compacted and proof-rolled to design bearing
- **Foundation excavation and backfill** — footings, walls and structures, with proper backfill and compaction against the structure
- **Over-excavation and subgrade replacement** — where native material won't support the design load
- **Rock excavation** — excavation, ripping, breaking, trimming and disposal or on-site reuse
- **Topsoil stripping, stockpiling and respread**
- **Site drainage and grading** — surface drainage established so water leaves the site rather than sitting on it
- **Yard and laydown construction** — granular placement, shaping and compaction for heavy-traffic areas
- **Demolition and site clearance** — structure removal, segregated handling, disposal and site restoration
- **Trucking and material supply** — haul, import of granular and structural material, export of surplus and unsuitable material

## Section — Cut and fill balance

**Heading:** `Balancing Cut and Fill on Site`

```
The single biggest controllable cost on an earthworks project is usually trucking.
Every cubic metre hauled off site and every cubic metre imported is money and
truck traffic that a better-balanced design might not have needed.

We look at that before we start. Where the material on site is suitable, we
reuse it — cut placed as fill, rock crushed or used as structural material,
topsoil stockpiled for respread rather than disposed of and repurchased.

Where a site genuinely will not balance, we say so and price the haul honestly.
What we will not do is discover it at week three and arrive with a change
order.

If you are still at design stage, involve us early. Earthworks cost is set by
the grading design more than by the contractor, and a conversation before the
drawings are finished is usually worth more than any saving negotiated after
tender.
```

That last paragraph is a business development tool as much as page copy. It positions you as worth calling at design stage, which is where the relationship with the engineer gets built — and engineers control the bid list.

## Section — Compaction and documentation

**Heading:** `Compaction Testing and Documentation`

```
Compaction is the part of earthworks nobody can see once the work is covered,
and it is the part that causes the failures. Our quality control program is
built around BC MOTI standards and applies to every project regardless of
client.

- Fill placed in specified lift thicknesses, not whatever the machine can push
- Density testing at specified frequency, with results documented as the work proceeds
- Grade verified and recorded against design
- Materials confirmed against specification on delivery
- Proof rolling of pads and subgrade before the next trade arrives
- A complete record handed to the owner at close-out, not assembled from memory afterwards
```
