## Section — COR certification

**Heading:** `COR® Certification`

```
RAM Excavating holds a Certificate of Recognition (COR®) issued through the BC
Construction Safety Alliance. COR® is not a self-declaration — it is awarded
after an external audit of a company's occupational health and safety management
system, and it has to be maintained through ongoing audits.

For an owner, that means our safety program has been independently verified
rather than described in a brochure.
```

## Section — Our safety program

**Heading:** `What Our Program Actually Does on Site`

```
A safety program is only worth what gets done in the field. These are standard
on every RAM project:

**Site-specific orientation.** Every worker and every visitor is oriented to the
site before they start — hazards, controls, emergency procedures, muster points
and site rules. Documented, not verbal.

**Daily field level hazard assessments.** Crews complete an FLHA at the start of
each shift and revisit it when conditions change. Referencing the actual hazards
on that site, not a template filled in from memory.

**Toolbox and pre-job meetings.** Regular crew meetings covering the day's work,
its hazards and the controls, with a record of who attended.

**Emergency preparedness.** A project-specific emergency response plan for every
site, including remote sites where response times are long and evacuation has to
be planned rather than assumed.

**Safe work procedures.** Written procedures for the high-risk work we do —
excavation and trenching, shoring and trench entry, working around live
utilities, mobile equipment and ground worker interaction, traffic control,
confined space, and working alone.

**Competency and training.** Workers are trained and certified for the equipment
they operate and the tasks they perform, with certifications tracked and renewed
before they expire — not after.

**Inspections.** Regular documented site and equipment inspections, with
findings tracked to closure.

**Incident and near-miss reporting.** All incidents and near misses are reported
and investigated. Near misses matter as much as incidents — they are the
warnings you get before something serious happens, and a company that does not
collect them is throwing away its best information.

**Subcontractor management.** Subcontractors on RAM projects are evaluated on
safety before award and work under a compliance agreement on site. Our standards
do not stop at our own payroll.
```

That near-miss paragraph is worth keeping as written. It is the kind of thing that tells an experienced safety manager the program is real, because it is what a mature program actually believes and an immature one never mentions.

```
WorkSafeBC standing         In good standing — clearance letter available on request
Experience rating           [[ IF FAVOURABLE, STATE IT ]]
```

**On publishing numbers.** Prequalification packages will ask for these anyway, so the only question is whether a buyer sees them on your website before or after they have shortlisted someone else. If your figures are good, publishing them is a competitive advantage very few regional contractors take. If a particular year was poor, publish a three-year average or a rolling figure rather than nothing — a range with context beats a blank, and a blank reads as something being hidden. If you would rather not publish any figures, replace this section with: *"Full safety statistics and our WorkSafeBC clearance letter are available on request for prequalification and tender purposes."* That is still better than an empty table.

**If you are not registered in ISNetworld, ComplyWorks or Avetta, that is worth acting on.** Those networks are the front door for mine, oil and gas and large industrial work — many of those buyers will not accept a bid from a contractor who is not registered, regardless of how good the safety record is. Being COR certified already puts you through the hardest part; the registrations mainly make you visible to buyers who are already looking. Happy to work through which ones are worth the fee for the work you actually want.
