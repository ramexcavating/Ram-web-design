This page is written for your crews, not for buyers. Plain, short, and useful on a phone with one bar of signal at the end of a shift — that is the whole design brief.

**Add `noindex` to this page** if your builder allows it (usually an SEO setting labelled "hide from search engines"). There is no reason for it to appear in Google results, and keeping it out avoids a buyer landing on an internal page.

---

## Decide first: should this page be protected?

Three options. Pick one before you build it.

| Option | Good for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Fully public** | Forms, policies, contacts, general info | Anyone can read it, including competitors. Fine for most of what goes here |
| **Password protected** (one shared password) | Everything above plus internal documents | One password for all staff, so it leaks eventually and has to be changed. Most builders support this |
| **Public page, gated documents** | Best of both | Page itself is public; sensitive documents sit behind a link to your Microsoft 365 / SharePoint where staff already have accounts |

**Recommended: the third.** You are already on Microsoft 365, so your staff have logins and your documents already live in SharePoint or OneDrive. A public page that links into SharePoint means proper per-person access, no shared password to leak, and no second place to keep documents up to date. `[[ DECIDE: which option, and if gating documents, get the SharePoint links ]]`

**Nothing containing personal information about an employee goes on this page**, protected or not — no crew lists with phone numbers, no certification matrix with individual names, no incident details. Those belong in SharePoint behind individual logins.

## What NOT to put on this page

- Employee names with phone numbers or addresses
- Individual training or certification records
- Incident reports or investigation details
- Client contracts, tender documents or pricing
- Anything containing a third party's personal information

All of it belongs in SharePoint behind individual logins, not on a web page — shared-password protection is not adequate for personal information, and BC privacy legislation treats employee personal information seriously.
