## Section — What we do

**Heading:** `HDPE Fusion and Installation Scope`

- **Butt fusion** — heat fusion of HDPE pipe across the range of municipal and industrial diameters
- **Electrofusion** — couplers, saddles and fittings where a butt fusion is not practical
- **Sidewall and saddle fusion** — service connections and branch outlets on live and new mains
- **Mechanical and flanged connections** — transitions to valves, fittings and dissimilar pipe materials
- **Pipe stringing, handling and fusion on site** — long strings fused above ground and installed in one pull where the site allows
- **Fusion for water and sewer mains** — potable water, forcemain and pressure sewer
- **Mine dewatering and water management lines** — pumped discharge, transfer and pond lines
- **Industrial process and utility lines** — process water, cooling water, chemical transfer and fire protection
- **Gas and utility conduit** where applicable
- **Trenchless carrier pipe** — HDPE carrier fused and pulled through casing on bored crossings
- **Repairs and tie-ins** on existing HDPE systems

## Section — Procedure and records

**Heading:** `Fusion Procedure and Joint Records`

```
Every joint we make is done to a documented fusion procedure — correct heater
temperature, correct facing and alignment, correct interfacial pressure, correct
heat soak and cool times for the pipe size and material, and no shortcuts on
cool time because the crew wants to move.

We record the joints. For a project of any size the owner gets a joint log
rather than an assurance that it was all done properly.

- Technicians trained and certified to fuse the pipe and fittings on the project
- Fusion equipment calibrated and maintained
- Procedure matched to the pipe DR, material and diameter
- Joint records kept and handed over at close-out
- Trial and test joints where the specification calls for them
- Pressure testing to owner specification after installation

Weather matters more with fusion than with most pipe work. Cold, wind and rain
all affect a joint, and we plan and shelter for it rather than fusing through
conditions the procedure does not allow — which is the most common reason a
fusion job goes wrong in this part of the country.
```

That final paragraph is genuinely persuasive to anyone technical. Cold-weather fusion is a real problem in central and northern BC, and a contractor who raises it unprompted sounds like one who has dealt with it.

## Section — Why HDPE

**Heading:** `Where HDPE Makes Sense`

```
Not every line should be HDPE, and we will say so. Where it does make sense
the advantages are substantial:

- **Fully restrained.** No thrust blocking, no restrained joints, nothing to pull apart at a bend
- **No leaks at joints.** A fused joint is a continuous section of pipe, so there is no gasket to fail
- **Ground movement tolerance.** Flexes rather than cracks — useful in unstable and frost-susceptible ground
- **Corrosion and scale resistance.** Long service life in aggressive water and soil conditions
- **Trenchless friendly.** Can be pulled through a bore or slip-lined into existing pipe
- **Long strings.** Fewer joints, faster installation where there is room to string and fuse above ground
- **Abrasion resistance.** Which is why it dominates mine dewatering and tailings service
```
