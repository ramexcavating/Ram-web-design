## Section — Working inside an operating plant

**Heading:** `Building Inside a Running Facility`

```
Most industrial civil work happens inside a plant that cannot stop producing
to accommodate a contractor. That constraint shapes everything — access,
sequencing, hot work, isolations, permit systems, and how much can realistically
be achieved in a shutdown window.

We plan around it:

- Work sequenced to the plant's shutdown and turnaround schedule
- Our crews work under the plant's permit-to-work, isolation and lockout systems
- Site-specific orientation and plant inductions completed before mobilisation
- Access and laydown planned so plant traffic and emergency routes stay clear
- Existing buried services located and hand-exposed before machine excavation
- Realistic shutdown scoping — we would rather tell you a five-day scope will not
  fit a three-day window than find out with the plant down
```

That last line is deliberately blunt. Plant and facility managers have all lost a turnaround to a contractor who over-promised, and it is the concern they carry into every tender.
