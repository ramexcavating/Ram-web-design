## Section — What we build

**Heading:** `Road and Highway Scope`

- **Public highway construction** — new construction, realignment, widening and rehabilitation to BC MOTI specification
- **Grade and subgrade** — clearing, grubbing, stripping, cut and fill, subgrade preparation and proof rolling
- **Granular base and surfacing** — subbase and base course placement, shaping and compaction to specified density
- **Road drainage** — ditching, culverts, cross-drains, catch basins, storm systems and outfall protection
- **Resource and access roads** — forestry, mine and industrial road construction, including in steep and remote terrain
- **Haul roads** — built and maintained for loaded truck traffic
- **Rock excavation and rock cuts** — excavation, trimming, scaling and slope treatment
- **Retaining structures and slope stabilisation** — MSE walls, riprap, bank protection, erosion control
- **Bridge and structure approaches** — approach fills, abutment excavation and backfill
- **Guardrail, signage and safety works** — installation and reinstatement
- **Road maintenance and rehabilitation** — regrading, resurfacing, drainage restoration, pothole and washout repair

## Section — BC MOTI standards

**Heading:** `Built to BC MOTI Standards — On Every Project`

```
Working for the BC Ministry of Transportation and Infrastructure means being
held to some of the most demanding construction standards in the country:
documented compaction and density testing, verified grade tolerances, specified
materials confirmed on delivery, and a paper trail that stands up to audit.

We built our quality control program around that standard — and then applied
it to everything. A municipal road, a mine haul road and a resource access
road all get the same testing and documentation regime as an MOTI contract.

For a public-sector owner that means a contractor already fluent in the
standards you administer. For a private owner it means you are getting
provincial-standard quality control on a project that did not require it.
```

## Section — Traffic and the public

**Heading:** `Working Under Live Traffic`

```
Highway and road work happens with the public driving through it. Traffic
management is not an afterthought on these projects — it is a large part of
what the owner is buying, and it is where most of the public complaints and
most of the serious risk sit.

- Traffic management plans prepared and permitted before mobilisation
- Certified traffic control personnel and correct signage, tapers and devices
- Single-lane alternating, detours and full closures planned with the owner and communicated ahead of time
- Night and off-peak work where daytime closures would be unacceptable
- Public and emergency service access maintained through the work
- Work zone reviewed daily as the work front moves — a plan set on day one and never revisited is how work zones become dangerous
```

## Section — Remote and resource roads

**Heading:** `Resource Roads and Remote Terrain`

```
Not every road we build has a centreline stripe. Forestry, mine and industrial
access roads in central and northern BC come with their own problems: steep
grades, unstable ground, watercourse crossings, long haul distances for
material, and a construction window closed by weather.

We build these routinely, and we plan the access and the material sourcing
before award rather than discovering the problem afterwards. Where a
watercourse crossing is involved, we work inside the permit conditions and
timing windows — see our dewatering and environmental page for how we handle
sediment and fisheries-sensitive work.
```
